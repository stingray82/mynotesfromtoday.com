The value of a small experiment

A reminder that trying one small thing can teach more than planning an entire system.

Tinkering9 October 20262 min read

A useful experiment does not need to be impressive. It needs a question you can answer and a stopping point you can recognise.

When a new tool or idea looks promising, the temptation is to redesign everything around it. A smaller test makes the result easier to see. Give it one job, note what happened, and keep the parts that genuinely helped.

Keep the evidence close

Write down the starting point, the change, and the outcome while they are still fresh. Include the awkward bits. They are often the most useful part when you return to the idea later.

This is the kind of note I want to collect here: modest experiments, honestly described, with room for a second attempt.
